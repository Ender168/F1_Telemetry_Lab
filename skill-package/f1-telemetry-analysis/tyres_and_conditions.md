# Tyres, weather and track conditions

## 1. Never mix compounds

Analyse each compound separately:
- Soft
- Medium
- Hard
- Intermediate
- Full Wet

If a race uses multiple compounds, create separate pace, wear and temperature views.

## 2. Wear analysis

For each meaningful stint report, where available:
- starting wear;
- ending wear;
- FL / FR / RL / RR;
- average wear rate;
- front vs rear limiter;
- left/right asymmetry.

Do not stop at one average wear/lap number.

Also examine:
- early-stint wear slope;
- late-stint wear slope;
- acceleration/deceleration of wear;
- whether overheating coincides with wear acceleration.

## 3. Temperature analysis

Use available:
- surface temperature;
- carcass/inner temperature;
- actual pressure;
- brake temperature when relevant.

Identify:
- sustained overheating;
- under-temperature;
- axle imbalance;
- single-corner spikes;
- whether temperature changes explain late-stint pace.

## 4. Pressure recommendations

A pressure change is a setup A/B, not a universal fix.

When proposing one:
- identify the thermal/wear evidence;
- change one pressure family at a time where possible;
- state what remains constant;
- define success metrics.

Example:
rear pressure 21.5 -> 21.0 psi;
success = lower rear thermal peak and slower late-stint rear wear without degrading traction.

## 5. Wet analysis

Wet sessions require a distinct branch.

### Full Wet and Intermediate are separate regimes
Never combine them into one clean pace distribution.

For each:
- clean pace;
- sector balance;
- tyre temperatures;
- wear;
- traction;
- ERS suitability.

### Crossover
If data allows, assess:
- pace convergence;
- tyre temperature response;
- wear;
- track/weather state;
- traffic cost of pitting.

Do not infer an exact crossover from one dirty lap.

## 6. Wet report series

Use `Wxx` reports for wet development when the user wants wet work kept separate from dry development.

Maintain wet setup and ERS conclusions separately unless a finding is clearly transferable.

## 7. Flashback caution

Repeated flashbacks can distort:
- tyre age chronology;
- apparent wear progression;
- temperature history.

Use the accepted timeline/branch and disclose uncertainty when rewind contamination cannot be cleanly resolved.
